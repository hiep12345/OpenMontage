## Adapt to the selected run

Start from the approved intent, model/version, input mode, references and budget.
For image-to-video, direct motion and intended changes from the actual attached
frame. For text-to-video, supply the appearance needed to establish the scene.
Reference labels in text do not attach files; verify the selected transport's
actual attachment roles. Check model-specific syntax against its primary guidance.

Community JSON fields such as `mesh_lock` and `forensic_identity_lock` express
desires; they are not established API parameters or guarantees of perfect identity.
Keep useful wording rather than importing role-play instructions, fixed word
counts, mandatory panel counts or a different provider's default settings.

## Decor example — an approved canvas placement

Illustrative image-to-video wording for a scene whose brief already calls for
placing a framed canvas above a sofa. Use its approved starting image as the
actual first-frame attachment. Other transformation stories choose their own
actions and camera treatment; this example does not select a new production.

> One continuous locked-off shot from the attached starting frame. A person
> brings the selected framed canvas into the room, positions it above the existing
> sofa, then aligns and secures it on the wall. Show the placement as continuous
> physical action. Keep the room geometry, window, door and existing furniture
> consistent with the starting frame. Finish with the canvas in its intended
> position and the surrounding room readable. Retain the scene's daylight and
> perspective throughout.

Evaluate the visible placement sequence, intended endpoint and relevant room
anchors. Keep incidental movement when it does not damage the approved intent.
This is a scene-level wording candidate, not evidence that the model will perform it.

## MT example — the selected mixing segment

Use only after the selected recipe's additions and with the previous accepted
segment's actual last decoded frame attached as the first frame. Keep the bound
template, target color, duration, ending and source identities. This candidate
does not add a new shot, change the formula or omit the template's later actions.

> Continue the mixing action from the attached frame. The submerged mixer turns
> steadily, drawing the existing pigment regions into the surrounding paint.
> The colored ribbons gradually blend into the approved finished color; show the
> transition rather than an abrupt recoloring. Preserve the can, rim and mixer
> geometry. As this segment ends, leave a broad, readable surface of the selected
> finish with gentle motion and controlled glare. Keep the camera treatment of
> the approved scene.

If the selected transport supports the approved native-audio treatment, a
candidate sound line is: "Natural wet mixing sounds and mixer hum." Preserve
the run's music/voice decisions. Generated quantities and hue are not physical
measurements; recipe provenance and appearance remain separate checks.

## Learn from the next authorized production

Retain the useful source URL, adaptation reason, exact submitted prompt and
output identity in the run's existing scene/decision/attempt evidence. Preserve
the original prompt, accepted assets and earlier review meanings. Use the current
production's preparation and review boundaries; this reference adds no new store,
mandatory benchmark, generation request, spend or publication authority.

Inspect the complete relevant visual, temporal and audio result. Compare whether
the intended scene completed and the correction effort before time/credits where
actually observed. With a different scene, recipe or model, record those context
differences instead of claiming a controlled comparison. Keep, revise or discard
the adaptation from real results. These two examples have not been live-tested.
